# Mayday Dispatch editorial standards

## Purpose and authority

Mayday Dispatch separates acquisition, analysis, draft preparation, editorial
review, release authorization, and public publication. Research Studio and
Overwatch can submit permitted material; Mayday3 can register and process
evidence. None can authorize a public release. A configured Dispatch publisher
or administrator must review the exact revision and explicitly release it.
Analytical completion, a ready state, a scheduled job, or a deployment does not
publish content.

These standards govern public work. The fact that information is available to
Mayday internally does not make it accurate, sufficiently supported, lawful to
publish, or appropriate for public disclosure.

## Source verification and evidence sufficiency

- Identify the origin, date, and limitations of each material source. Preserve
  the source's own distinction between direct observation, attributed claim,
  and later interpretation.
- Prefer independently corroborated primary material when available. Multiple
  copies of one original source are not independent corroboration.
- Record contradictory, missing, stale, or methodologically limited evidence.
  Do not resolve material disagreement by omitting it without explanation.
- An editor assesses whether sources support the specific public claims, not
  merely whether citations exist. A citation is not proof of the claim.
- Do not expose private or internal evidence, raw collection data, access
  paths, credentials, non-public identifiers, or source identities without an
  authorized disclosure decision.
- Verify rights, attribution terms, privacy interests, safety consequences,
  and applicable legal restrictions before using third-party text, images,
  datasets, or media.

## Observation, inference, and uncertainty

Separate what a source directly records from what Dispatch infers. Attribute
claims to their sources, state analytical assumptions, and explain meaningful
uncertainty, missing data, alternative explanations, and limits on confidence.
Do not convert an estimate or incomplete signal into a categorical fact.

Forecasts describe conditional scenarios, assumptions, indicators, and an
appropriate time horizon. A forecast is not a promise or a statement of
certainty. State what evidence would change the assessment when that can be
done without disclosing restricted material.

## Publication categories

- **Global Monitor**: situation-oriented monitoring and concise reporting on
  current conditions. Time, geographic scope, source freshness, and caveats
  should be clear.
- **Analysis**: longer interpretive or research-based work that explains its
  question, evidence, method, and limits.
- **Forecast**: forward-looking observations, scenarios, assumptions, and
  indicators, with uncertainty stated prominently.

These labels describe the work; none grants publication permission or changes
the evidence, privacy, rights, or release requirements.

## Readiness and release

Readiness is an editorial checkpoint, not a publication event. Reviewers
confirm the article is complete, sources and evidence are sufficient, public
visibility is intentional, sensitive information is excluded, and the public
projection is valid. A separate publisher authorizes release of the exact
expected version. The application records an immutable public snapshot,
content digest, evidence-validation result, publisher, operation, and audit
correlation in a release manifest.

Edits after release are staged. A correction requires a clear public correction
note; a substantive update requires a clear public update note. The revised
version remains unpublished until reviewed and explicitly released. Published
material is withdrawn using the archive action, which removes it from public
queries and the sitemap; lockdown does not hide already released material.

## Corrections, updates, and archives

Promptly assess credible reports of material error. Preserve the prior
revision, record the correction decision, and describe the changed information
in the public notice without exposing restricted details. Do not silently
replace a public claim. Substantive new analysis is an update, not a correction;
explain its scope and keep the previous public snapshot live until the new
revision is authorized.

Archive material when it is superseded, no longer suitable for public access,
or subject to a withdrawal decision. Record the reason internally. For a
possible safety, privacy, rights, or legal issue, escalate to the designated
publisher and incident owner; use publication lockdown to stop new releases
while the issue is assessed. Archival and restoration decisions remain human
editorial actions.

## Sensitive-source handling

Keep acquired source collections, client-provided material, proprietary Mayday
analysis, credentials, and operational records outside the public asset tree.
Use the least revealing public citation that still enables fair attribution.
Do not place restricted content in a public notice, public URL, image caption,
metadata field, alt text, table, map label, or revision summary. Escalate
uncertain disclosure decisions rather than treating a public visibility flag
as a substitute for source review.

## Roles and accountability

Editors prepare and review revisions; publishers authorize public releases;
operators control release lockdown and recovery. Separate operator and
publisher identities. Administrators have broad application authority and
should not be used as routine deployment identities. Record the decision and
responsible role in the publication workflow before each release.
